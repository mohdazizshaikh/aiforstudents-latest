METHODOLOGY

What our feedback measures—and what it does not

The 40 available game modules cover eight broad exploration categories. They use finite rule-generated question banks, reusable input formats and unscored reflection prompts. Four age bands provide different complexity in many tasks; they are not standardized, normed or psychometrically equated.

Task results

Scored items compare user input with a locally defined answer key. Reflective items are not given numeric aptitude scores. A completed task shows what was attempted and the explanation or prompt, but does not establish a stable trait.

Repeat avoidance and limits

Each game and age band offers a finite pool of 20 task records in the current prototype. Previously seen IDs are preferred less until the eligible pool is exhausted; exact questions or concepts may eventually recur, particularly if storage is cleared or content versions change.

Growth comparisons

Practice-to-practice score comparisons require equal format/content version and the same broad task families. Even then, tasks are not necessarily difficulty-equated. Feedback describes this practice only; it cannot quantify changes in IQ or predict professional aptitude.

Future Explorer

Exploration pathways are reordered only by interests learners explicitly select; scores and reflections do not automatically decide career fits. Offline missions marked done by a student are self-reported, not externally verified.

Review status

Automated tests catch formatting, answer-key and some translation-token issues. They do not replace professional educational review, bilingual semantic review, accessibility testing, research validation, or legal/child-safety review. All challenge records currently remain unapproved for public child-directed assessment.
